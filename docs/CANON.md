# The Missing Person: Canon

**Full spoilers.** This is the writers' source of truth. It lives outside `src/` so none of it ships with the game. If in-game content disagrees with this file, the content is wrong, unless the contradiction is listed here as intentional.

All people, places, organizations, websites and records are fictional. In-game addresses use `.example` domains. IP addresses use the reserved documentation ranges (`192.0.2.x`, `198.51.100.x`, `203.0.113.x`).

---

## 1. The story in one page

**The theme:** nobody forged Eleanor's disappearance on their own. Almost everyone edited one small thing, and each of them believed theirs was the harmless one.

In the early hours of 15 November 2013, a fire at the disused Harrow Mill killed Peter Aldous, a former mill worker sleeping in the boiler house. It was started by accident by three drunk teenagers. One was Callum Reed, 17, son of DI Thomas Reed, a widower for whom Callum was all that remained. Callum phoned his father. Reed reached the mill at **02:45**, got the boys out and drove Callum home. He made an anonymous 999 call at **03:14**. He did not know anyone was inside. Those 29 minutes probably cost Aldous his life, and Reed then ran the investigation himself.

Eleanor Vale had a motion-triggered wildlife camera in the wheel house, watching barn owls. It recorded all of this. In February 2014 she innocently handed the original card to the officer in charge of the fire inquiry, DI Reed. She kept a copy. In September 2014 she photographed a car at Heritage Day, and its plate matched the partial plate in the frame. She knew who the man was.

Eleanor prepared carefully. She built a **dead man's switch**: if nobody typed a password into a page on her site for 30 days, the site would post a warning and, seven days later, email everything she had to three addresses. She told her friend Sarah Bennett, a reporter, about the frames but not who was in them. Sarah did what reporters do and rang the officer in charge for comment. That officer was Reed.

On Friday 17 October 2014, Reed asked Eleanor to meet him at the mill, promising to return the original card. She went because the switch made her feel safe. On the fire-damaged gallery above the flooded wheel pit, the boards gave way and she fell. Reed searched for about 50 minutes and found no one. For the second time, he did not make the call.

He took her bag and used her phone to fake a departure. Then, because the division was small and he made sure of it, he led the search for her.

Everyone else made it worse in small, human ways:

- **Daniel Cross**, her ex, was tracking her phone. He followed her to the mill road, saw a parked car, left, and lied about it to hide the stalking.
- **Sarah** lied about when they last spoke, sitting across a table from the man she was afraid of.

**The twist:** the switch never fired, because people looking for her kept typing the password. It stopped working as an alarm and became a memorial. When Sarah restored the site in October 2026, the switch woke up and posted the warning Eleanor had written twelve years earlier:

> I know you're looking for me. You shouldn't be.

It sounds like a threat from beyond the grave. It is a practical instruction. *If you look for me, you will find the word, and if you type the word, the system thinks I'm fine. Stop looking and let it run.*

**The player** has typed the word by then, because the game made it look like progress.

## 3. Characters

### Eleanor Margaret Vale

Born 3 April 1988. Lives at 14B Weir Lane, Ashford (the "yellow house"). Works at Copperplate Print on Bridge Street. Amateur photographer and web designer. Runs `eleanorvale.example`, titled "~ eleanor's corner of the web ~". Dry, curious and meticulous. She documents things because she doesn't trust memory, including her own.

- **Voice:** warm, self-mocking, precise about small facts. Two spaces after every full stop, even in texts. Writes "okay", never "ok". Never signs off with a kiss.
- **Sample:** "The roof is mostly a suggestion now.  I stayed for two hours and got one good photograph and a lot of pigeon on my coat."
- **Not a saint:** she edits too. She disguises photos, plants anonymous messages, and keeps secrets from the friend she relies on. Telling Sarah only part of it led directly to the phone call that exposed her.

### Daniel Cross

Born 1985. Freelance programmer. With Eleanor from March 2011 to 19 January 2014, when she ended it. He set up her hosting and shared her passwords, then kept them. He installed a tracker (FindMate) on her phone and a keylogger on her laptop "for backups, and because she loses everything".

- **Guilty of:** stalking, lying to police, deleting evidence of his own behavior, and failing to say where she went.
- **Innocent of:** anything that happened at the mill.
- **Voice:** all lowercase, no apostrophes, long run-on sentences. Tender and controlling at once.
- **Sample:** "im not checking up on you i just think its weird you changed the wifi and didnt say, tell me youre ok and ill leave it"
- **2026:** 41, a father, living away from Ashford.

### Sarah Bennett

Born 1988. Eleanor's friend since school. Reporter at the *Ashford Courier* from 2010 until it closed in 2018, then freelance.

- **Guilty of:** lying in her statement, deleting the last email, and in 2026 redacting her own call from the archive she assembled.
- **Why:** on 22 October 2014 her statement was taken by Reed, whom she already knew to be the man in the frame. She was afraid of him, and ashamed that her call had exposed Eleanor.
- **What she did try:** she took it to her editor, who refused to run it, and sent an anonymous complaint to Police Standards, which went nowhere.
- **Voice:** clean, quick, em dashes, and the habit of correcting herself mid-thought.
- **Sample:** "She was fine — no, that's what I've been saying for twelve years. She was frightened and I was busy."

### Thomas Reed

Born 9 June 1957, died 3 March 2026. Detective Inspector, Ashford Division, Westmarch Constabulary. Retired 31 March 2017. His wife Anne died in 2012. Courteous and well liked by people who dealt with him, including Eleanor.

- **Guilty of:** the delayed call, the cover-up of the fire, failing to report Eleanor's fall, faking her departure, and tampering with the case file.
- **Not guilty of:** planning to hurt anyone. He went to the mill intending to talk her out of it, and he has never been sure what he would have done if she had refused.
- **Voice:** formal, passive, faintly old-fashioned. Dates as `17.10.14`, 24-hour times with a dot (`20.41`), "whilst".
- **Sample:** "Attended from home 03.52. Scene already in hand. I record this for completeness."

### UNKNOWN USER

Not a person. Eleanor wrote the guestbook script, and if the name field was blank it displayed `UNKNOWN USER`. Every such post has `uid=0`. Players first read it as one sinister account. Inspecting the posts shows different IPs and browsers, and finally that "the account" was never an account. Section 7 lists who wrote what.

## 4. Places

- **Ashford:** small town on the River Ashe.
- **Harrow Mill:** disused textile mill on Mill Road. Key parts are the drying loft (fire started here), boiler house (Aldous died here), and wheel house (owls, camera, flooded wheel pit, upper gallery). A culvert runs from the pit to the river.
- **Wheelhouse Court:** apartments built on the site, opened 2018. The wheel house was kept as a "heritage feature", and the pit is now under a glass panel in the lobby.
- **14B Weir Lane:** Eleanor's flat, in the yellow house.
- **Mill Road phone box:** source of the 03:14 call in 2013 and the 12:07 call in 2014.

## 8. Puzzles

### Puzzle 1: The password in the blog

- **Answer:** `TYTOALBA` (the barn owl's scientific name).
- **Mechanic:** first letters of the eight post titles, in date order:

| Date | Title |
| --- | --- |
| 10 Mar 2012 | **T**esting, testing |
| 23 Sep 2012 | **Y**ellow house on Weir Lane |
| 16 Mar 2013 | **T**hings people leave behind |
| 17 Jun 2013 | **O**wl camera! |
| 24 Nov 2013 | **A**fter the fire |
| 11 Feb 2014 | **L**ost afternoon at the station |
| 12 Oct 2014 | **B**ackups (a boring post) |
| 15 Oct 2014 | **A**lmost done |

- **Hints:** "First things first" (the 9 Oct 2014 guestbook post, and again in "Backups"). "Owl camera!" names *Tyto alba* in passing, so a partial solve is confirmable.
- **Entry point:** a password box at `/owls/`, reached via a link in "Backups".
- **Wrong answer:** "That's not it.  Start at the beginning."
- **Unlocks:** the owl gallery and File Recovery.
- **Hidden effect:** this is a check-in. The log records it. The player finds out at Puzzle 4.

### Puzzle 2: The photograph's metadata

- **Subject:** "Blue hour, Harrow Mill", in the public gallery, captioned October 2013.
- **Discovery:** metadata shows `DateTimeOriginal 2013:11:15 02:52:07`, camera `TrailEye TC-12`, and `Software: GIMP`, modified 14 Oct 2014. Blue hour doesn't happen at 02:52. No such camera is used for her other gallery photos, but "Owl camera!" names it.
- **Solve:** the player flags the conflicting field and enters the real date, `15 Nov 2013`.
- **Follow-up:** zooming shows a partial plate `FX09 R…`, which matches the car behind the fire engine in the Heritage Day photo (`FX09 RDT`). A locked archive note lists `FX09 RDT` as DI Reed's vehicle.
- **Unlocks:** the frame as evidence. Reed's investigator notes in the archive.

### Puzzle 3: The deleted email

- **Source:** fragments in File Recovery, from the *Courier*'s mail archive. Sarah deleted her inbox copy in 2014, but rescued the archive when the paper closed in 2018 and could never bring herself to destroy it. Fragments must be put in order, using sentence logic and partial timestamps.
- **Reconstructed email:**

> **From:** Eleanor Vale **To:** Sarah Bennett **Thu 16 Oct 2014, 23:02**
>
> You should have told me before you rang them, but I should have told you why it mattered, so we'll call that even.
>
> It's him.  The man in the frame is Reed.  I've checked the plate twice.  You rang the one person in Ashford you couldn't ring.
>
> Don't put this in writing anywhere else.  Don't come round.  D still has my passwords so I'm doing this from the library in the morning if it doesn't send.
>
> I have told him nothing, and I've got something set up that doesn't need me to be brave.  First things first, like I said.
>
> Okay.  Go to bed.  It's only an owl camera.

- **The contradiction it reveals:** Sarah told police her last contact was the 15th, and she knew who Reed was when he took her statement. It also tells the player that **Sarah rang the station**, but the switchboard log in the archive shows no such call, only a black bar.
- **Unlocks:** the Hollis & Dunn letter in Messages, which contains the Puzzle 4 clue. Sarah's profile updates.

### Puzzle 4: The terminal code

- **Clue:** Hollis & Dunn's letter to Sarah: "Mr Reed left one instruction regarding the box: 'The code is the minute I should have picked up the telephone.'"
- **Deduction:** he arrived at 02:52 by EXIF. The camera runs 7 minutes fast. The real time is 02:45.
- **Answer:** `unlock 0245`.
- **Wrong answer feedback for `0252`:** "So close to what the camera thought." For `0314`: "That's the minute he did."
- **Unlocks:** `reed_box`, containing Reed's letter, Marsh's tally, the unredacted switchboard log and frames `0228` to `0240`. Also opens `site_restore`, the folder Sarah stored beside it, holding `checkin.php` and `checkin.log` from Daniel's backup.
- **The turn:** `checkin.php` shows what Puzzle 1 did:

```php
// If nobody types the word for 30 days: post the warning, wait 7, send everything.
// D has my passwords and sooner or later he'll go looking for me.
// So will anyone else who cares.
// If you're looking for me you'll find the word.  If you type the word it thinks I'm fine.
// So don't look.  Let it run.
```

## 10. Endings

All endings are filed from Case Conclusion, except the secret one. Case Conclusion opens after three documents are read.

### 1. THE OFFICIAL STORY

- **Condition:** player files "voluntary departure", at any point.
- **Sequence:** a clerical, quiet ending. The player's conclusion is added to the file with a stamp. The closure statement is reprinted with its own phrases repeated back. Eleanor's site goes offline a little while later, with a polite hosting error.
- **Reused clues:** whichever evidence the player had already found appears as "reviewed, no action".

### 2. THE WRONG PERSON

- **Condition:** player accuses Daniel or Sarah. Accusing Reed without Puzzle 5 is refused, with an explanation that the file doesn't yet support it.
- **Sequence (Daniel):** the player's case is persuasive. False alibi, stalkerware, key to the flat. The forum takes it from there. His name, his workplace, his child's school. - **Sequence (Sarah):** her career ends. She publishes a full admission of what she did, which reads like a confession to something bigger.
- **Reused clues:** every clue the player used, stated confidently and each technically true.
- **Emotional note:** complicity. Last UNKNOWN USER post: "Everything you said about them was true."

### 3. THE TRUTH

- **Condition:** Puzzles 1 to 5 solved, and the player names Reed as responsible.
- **Sequence:**
  1. The chain plays back in order, using the player's own board connections.
  2. Peter Aldous gets his name, and the coroner's "03:00" sits next to "02:45".
  3. Sarah's admission. She has replaced the dead addresses in the script with live ones.
  4. The pit under the lobby at Wheelhouse Court is drained. They find a camera strap and old boards. They do not find her.
  5. Callum Reed, 30, teacher, learns about the 29 minutes from the news on a Tuesday. He has done nothing since he was 17 except believe his father.
  6. **The last action.** Nothing is left to press. The switch will send everything in 30 days, as long as nobody types the word. The `/owls/` password box is still there and still works.
- **Mechanic:** a real 30-day timer based on the player's device clock, stored locally. If they come back after 30 days without typing the word, they find a final epilogue: "Sent." Typing the word at any point resets it, and the game says nothing about it.
- **Emotional note:** the hardest instruction in the game is to stop looking.

### 4. STILL ONLINE (secret)

- **Condition:** Puzzle 4 solved, plus all of:
  1. Read `checkin.log` in the terminal.
  2. Found frame `TE_0917` in the owl gallery (section 11).
  3. Found `/~eleanor/still/` (Easter egg 8).
  4. Ran the unlisted terminal command `checkin`, then typed the word, **after** reading the script's warning.
- **Sequence:** no case summary and no consequences. The log gains the player's line, with today's date. A minute later, it gains another, from a device nobody can account for. The guestbook gets a new post by UNKNOWN USER: the player's own words, taken from their notes. The visitor counter moves. Eleanor's site is shown one last time, and it looks fine. Nothing's wrong with it at all.
- **Rational reading:** Sarah's watching the logs and running one last experiment on the reader she picked, and she built the interface the notes were typed into.
- **Other readings:** left open.
- **Emotional note:** you knew what it would do, and you did it anyway. The question "who is UNKNOWN USER?" has a final answer: whoever's next.
- **Last line:** `next check-in due: the 17th`

## 14. Writing rules

1. **Ordinary first.** Most of the blog is about owls, bad landlords and pigeons. Disturbing details only land against normal ones.
2. **No character explains the plot.** Sarah's and Reed's confessions come late, and each is incomplete and self-serving.
3. **Reed is never a monster, and never excused.** Same for Daniel.
4. **Eleanor stays a person.** She's funny. Don't let her become a symbol, and don't let the game forget that she's missing.
5. **Name Peter Aldous.** Early records say "unidentified male". The player's attention is what restores him.
6. **Every clue has two jobs.** It connects to at least one other clue.
7. **Respect the voices** in section 3. A player should be able to identify a writer from punctuation alone.
8. **No jump scares, no gore.** The fall is never shown. The worst things here are a timestamp and a sentence in a guestbook.
9. **Keep the UI honest.** Horror comes from content. Game controls never lie about what they do, except the one moment that is the point: the password box in Puzzle 1.
