THE MISSING PERSON — COMPLETE AI CODER PROMPT

You are an experienced senior frontend developer, UI/UX designer,
narrative designer, and interactive fiction developer. Build a complete,
polished, browser-based mystery investigation game titled THE MISSING
PERSON.

Do not create only a landing page or a visual prototype. Build a fully
navigable, functional frontend experience with a coherent mystery,
interconnected clues, working puzzles, persistent progress, meaningful
interactions, and multiple endings.

The result should feel like discovering a forgotten personal website and
slowly realizing that its contents are connected to a real
disappearance. The experience should begin as a grounded true-crime
investigation and gradually become unsettling, psychological, and
ARG-like. Keep the supernatural interpretation ambiguous: the player
should never be completely certain whether something impossible is
happening or whether someone is manipulating the evidence.

All people, locations, organizations, websites, records, and events in
the game are fictional. Do not use real missing-person cases or real
personal information.

Build the project using:

-   React
-   TypeScript
-   Vite
-   Modern CSS (CSS modules or well-organized custom CSS)

-   LocalStorage for saving game progress and settings

Constraints:

-   This is a frontend-only project. Do not require a backend, database,

-   Store all game content and state locally in the project.
-   Persist player progress, discovered clues, solved puzzles, notes,

-   Include a way to reset the investigation and start a new game.
-   Do not access the user’s real filesystem, camera, microphone,
    location, or browser data. Any file system, terminal, browser, or

-   Do not create fake buttons that do nothing. Every visible
    interactive control must work.
-   Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
-   Use reusable React components and a clean, maintainable project
    structure.
-   Handle loading, empty, locked, completed, and error states
    thoughtfully.
-   Make the app usable with keyboard navigation and visible focus
    states.
-   Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

The application should feel like a real product, not a generic dashboard
template.

2. CORE STORY

Case #0917 — The Disappearance of Eleanor Vale

The game takes place in the fictional town of Ashford.

Eleanor Vale, age 26, disappeared on 17 October 2014. She was an amateur
web designer and photographer who maintained a small personal website.
Her disappearance was investigated, but the case eventually became
inactive.

In 2026, someone discovers that Eleanor’s old personal website is online
again. Most of it looks like an ordinary, outdated website from the
early 2010s. However, a new post has appeared, despite the website
supposedly being abandoned for more than a decade:

  “I know you’re looking for me. You shouldn’t be.”

The player enters the investigation by exploring Eleanor’s website,
archived police documents, emails, messages, photographs, recovered
files, and other digital traces.

At first, the evidence seems to point toward a familiar missing-person
case. As the player investigates, inconsistencies begin to emerge:

-   Some documents contain dates that do not match other records.
-   Certain photographs appear to have been modified.
-   An email seems to have been deleted and partially reconstructed.
-   A guestbook account called UNKNOWN USER posted messages after
    Eleanor disappeared.
-   Different people remember the same events in contradictory ways.
-   Some files appear to have been accessed or modified years after the
    case was closed.
-   The new message on Eleanor’s website may not have been written by
    Eleanor.

The central mystery is not simply “Where is Eleanor?” The player must
determine what happened, which evidence was manipulated, who had access
to it, and who is currently communicating through the old website.

Narrative principles

-   Build a coherent mystery with a definite internal chronology and a
    canonical explanation.
-   Do not reveal the answer too early.
-   Every major clue should have a purpose and connect to at least one
    other clue.
-   Red herrings are welcome, but they must be explainable.
-   Keep the player uncertain about whether the unsettling events are
    supernatural, psychological, or deliberate manipulation.
-   The story should become progressively more disturbing without
    relying on cheap jumpscares.
-   Use environmental storytelling, inconsistencies, subtle changes,
    hidden content, and unreliable records.
-   The player should be able to form theories before reaching an
    ending.
-   Make all character motivations understandable, even when they are
    suspicious.
-   Do not make every character obviously evil.

3. CHARACTERS

Create detailed fictional character profiles. Include a short biography,
relationship to Eleanor, possible motive, relevant timeline, and
information that becomes available as the player discovers evidence.

Eleanor Vale

-   Age: 26 at the time of disappearance.
-   Amateur photographer and web designer.
-   Maintained a personal website between approximately 2012 and 2014.
-   Interested in abandoned places, photography, old websites, and

-   Her blog entries and photographs should reveal her personality

Daniel Cross

-   Eleanor’s former boyfriend.
-   A programmer with technical knowledge.
-   Had access to some of Eleanor’s accounts and devices.
-   His messages should be ambiguous: sometimes caring, sometimes

Sarah Bennett

-   Eleanor’s close friend.
-   A journalist.
-   Had personal conversations with Eleanor shortly before the
    disappearance.
-   Some of her statements conflict with other records.
-   Her reasons for withholding information should be gradually
    revealed.

Thomas Reed

-   Former police investigator associated with the original case.
-   His notes contain inconsistencies and unexplained omissions.
-   He may have been pressured to close the investigation or may have
    had his own reasons for doing so.

UNKNOWN USER

-   An anonymous account in Eleanor’s guestbook.
-   Posted messages both before and after the disappearance.
-   The account’s identity is one of the important mysteries.
-   Messages should become increasingly unsettling as the player
    progresses.

Create believable dialogue and distinct writing styles for each

4. VISUAL DIRECTION AND ART STYLE

The visual design is one of the most important parts of this project.
Make it atmospheric, distinctive, polished, and memorable.

Overall visual identity

Create a hybrid of:

1.  A cinematic detective investigation interface.
2.  A forgotten personal website from the early 2000s / early 2010s.
3.  A police digital archive.
4.  A psychological horror experience.

The player should feel as if they are moving between different digital
spaces that were never meant to be connected.

Color palette

Use a restrained, atmospheric palette:

-   Charcoal and near-black backgrounds.
-   Muted burgundy and dark red accents.
-   Faded olive and desaturated green.
-   Aged paper, dirty beige, and off-white for documents.
-   Muted grey-blue for secondary information.
-   Very limited bright red, reserved for important warnings or
    disturbing discoveries.

Avoid oversaturated colors and generic neon cyberpunk aesthetics.

Typography

Use typography intentionally:

-   Elegant or editorial typography for major headings.
-   Readable sans-serif typography for interface elements.
-   Monospace typography for terminal output, metadata, logs, and
    technical documents.
-   Old-web typography for Eleanor’s personal website.
-   Distinct typography treatments for police documents, emails, and
    recovered files.

Maintain strong readability. Horror atmosphere must never make important
text difficult to read.

Texture and effects

Use subtle effects, not constant visual noise:

-   Film grain.
-   CRT scanlines in selected areas.
-   Slight analog distortion.
-   Occasional subtle glitch effects.
-   Faded paper texture.
-   Soft shadows and layered panels.
-   Typewriter-like text reveals in selected moments.
-   Small visual imperfections that make the archived website feel old.
-   Carefully timed transitions and micro-interactions.

Glitch effects should be rare and meaningful. Do not make the entire
interface constantly flicker.

Layout and motion

-   Create a strong visual hierarchy.
-   Use deliberate spacing and alignment.
-   Use layered panels, side navigation, tabs, cards, and document
    viewers where appropriate.
-   Use smooth transitions between investigation sections.
-   Add hover states and subtle interaction feedback.
-   Use animations to reinforce discovery and tension.
-   Avoid excessive motion and respect reduced-motion preferences.
-   Make the design responsive for desktop, tablet, and mobile.

The experience should look professionally art-directed. Do not use a
generic admin dashboard layout.

6. PUZZLES AND INVESTIGATION MECHANICS

Create at least 5 meaningful puzzles. They must be fully functional and
integrated into the story.

Each puzzle should have: - A clear discovery path. - A logical
solution. - Feedback when the player enters an incorrect answer. - A
satisfying result when solved. - A clue or unlock that affects the wider
investigation. - Persistent solved state.

Required puzzle concepts:

Puzzle 1 — Hidden Password in the Blog

A password or code is hidden across one or more blog entries. The player
must notice a pattern in dates, wording, formatting, or repeated
details.

The solution should unlock a protected area or file.

Puzzle 2 — Photograph Metadata

The player inspects a photograph and discovers that its metadata
contains an inconsistency or hidden information.

The clue should connect the photograph to another event or character.

Puzzle 3 — Deleted Email Reconstruction

The player finds fragments of a deleted email and must reconstruct the
correct message or missing information.

The completed email should reveal a significant contradiction or lead.

Puzzle 4 — Terminal Code

The player discovers a code through other parts of the investigation and
uses it in the simulated terminal to unlock a file or archive.

Puzzle 5 — Final Evidence Connections

The player must connect several pieces of evidence on the evidence board
to establish a coherent explanation of what happened.

Do not make puzzles arbitrary. Their solutions must be inferable from
information already available in the game.

You may add more puzzles if they improve the experience, but prioritize
quality and narrative integration over quantity.

Implement a coherent progression system.

Track at least: - Discovered evidence. - Read documents. - Viewed
photographs. - Discovered messages. - Solved puzzles. - Unlocked pages
and files. - Evidence board connections. - Player notes. - Timeline
discoveries. - Ending reached. - Settings such as sound and motion
preferences.

Use LocalStorage to persist all relevant state.

Requirements: - Progress must survive refreshes and browser restarts. -
Avoid losing progress when navigating between sections. - Provide a
clear reset/new-game action with confirmation. - Make locked content
unlock consistently. - Prevent duplicate evidence entries. - Ensure that
a player cannot accidentally bypass the intended progression through a
UI bug. - Keep the state model understandable and maintainable.

and meaningful interaction—not from loud effects or jumpscares.

The entire application must work on: - Desktop. - Tablet. - Mobile.

Requirements: - Adapt navigation for small screens. - Make evidence
